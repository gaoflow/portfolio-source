Steady Conduction 1-D technical report

Steady 1-D Conduction: the first discretised PDE

Objective

This study is the first time a partial differential equation is discretised and solved in this portfolio. The problem is deliberately small — steady heat conduction along a rod of length LL with uniform conductivity kk, optional uniform volumetric generation q‴q''', a fixed-temperature boundary at x=0x=0, and a convective tip at x=Lx=L — so that every step (discretisation, boundary-condition treatment, linear solve, verification) can be checked against a closed-form answer. The study asks four concrete questions:

  1. Does the finite-difference solution reproduce the exact solution?
  2. Does the error shrink at the expected rate under grid refinement?
  3. Does the discrete solution satisfy the global energy balance?
  4. Is the hand-written tridiagonal solver correct against a reference dense solve?

Governing equation and exact solution

Steady conduction with uniform properties and uniform generation reduces to

kd2Tdx2+q‴=0,T(0)=Tleft,−kdTdx|x=L=hc(T(L)−T∞).k\,\frac{d^2T}{dx^2}+q'''=0,\qquad T(0)=T_\text{left},\qquad -k\,\frac{dT}{dx}\bigg|_{x=L}=h_c\bigl(T(L)-T_\infty\bigr).

Integrating twice and applying both boundary conditions gives the closed form

T(x)=Tleft+C1x−q‴x22k,C1=q‴L+hcq‴L22k−hc(Tleft−T∞)k+hcL.T(x)=T_\text{left}+C_1 x-\frac{q'''\,x^2}{2k},\qquad C_1=\frac{q'''L+\dfrac{h_c\,q'''L^2}{2k}-h_c\,(T_\text{left}-T_\infty)}{k+h_cL}.

The exact solution is quadratic in xx. That fact has a direct consequence for verification, discussed below.

Discretisation

The rod is split into NN uniform cells with nodes xi=ihx_i=ih, h=L/Nh=L/N, i=0…Ni=0\ldots N.

  • Dirichlet node i=0i=0: the row is the identity, T0=TleftT_0=T_\text{left}.
  • Interior nodes i=1…N−1i=1\ldots N-1: central differences, kh2(Ti−1−2Ti+Ti+1)=−q‴i.\frac{k}{h^2}\left(T_{i-1}-2T_i+T_{i+1}\right)=-q'''_i.
  • Convective tip i=Ni=N: a half-control-volume balance couples the last interior face flux to the surface convection, −kh(TN−TN−1)+h2q‴N=hc(TN−T∞).-\frac{k}{h}\left(T_N-T_{N-1}\right)+\frac{h}{2}q'''_N=h_c\left(T_N-T_\infty\right).

The assembled system is tridiagonal and is solved with a Thomas algorithm implemented from scratch (forward elimination, back substitution, explicit zero-pivot guards). No library linear solve is used in the production path; numpy.linalg.solve appears only as an independent cross-check.

Verification

Exactness on the physical case

Central differences are exact for quadratics, and the half-cell Robin balance is also exact for quadratics (the face-flux truncation term −h2kT″-\tfrac{h}{2}kT'' is cancelled exactly by the half-cell generation term h2q‴\tfrac{h}{2}q'''). The discrete equations are therefore satisfied by the nodal values of the exact solution. At N=160N=160 the maximum nodal error against the closed form is 1.34×10−111.34\times10^{-11} K — roundoff, not truncation error. This is retained as a verification result in its own right: the scheme is consistent and correctly assembled, which is exactly the failure mode (sign errors, boundary-row mistakes) that a convergence study alone can mask.

Grid-convergence study

Because the physical solution is quadratic, a refinement study on it would measure roundoff, not discretisation error. The convergence study therefore uses the method of manufactured solutions: a sinusoidal source

q‴(x)=kA(πL)2sin⁡(πxL),Tm(x)=Tleft+C1x+Asin⁡(πxL),q'''(x)=kA\left(\tfrac{\pi}{L}\right)^2\sin\left(\tfrac{\pi x}{L}\right),\qquad T_m(x)=T_\text{left}+C_1x+A\sin\left(\tfrac{\pi x}{L}\right),

with C1C_1 chosen so that TmT_m satisfies the same Dirichlet and Robin conditions. Halving hh from N=20N=20 through N=160N=160 gives max nodal errors 4.81×10−2,1.20×10−2,3.00×10−3,7.51×10−44.81\times10^{-2},\ 1.20\times10^{-2},\ 3.00\times10^{-3},\ 7.51\times10^{-4} K — a least-squares observed order of 2.00022.0002, inside the declared band [1.8,2.2][1.8,\,2.2].

Energy closure

Summing the discrete cell balances telescopes the interior face fluxes and yields the global identity

(−kT1−T0h−h2q‴0)⏟flux in at x=0+∫0Lq‴dx⏟generation=hc(TN−T∞)⏟convected out,\underbrace{\left(-k\frac{T_1-T_0}{h}-\frac{h}{2}q'''_0\right)}_{\text{flux in at }x=0} +\underbrace{\int_0^L q'''\,dx}_{\text{generation}} =\underbrace{h_c\left(T_N-T_\infty\right)}_{\text{convected out}},

where the trapezoidal quadrature of q‴q''' matches the discrete source sum exactly. On the production case the relative residual is 1.59×10−121.59\times10^{-12}, below the 10−1010^{-10} gate. Note the physics: of the 10,00010{,}000 W/m² generated, only 1,5111{,}511 W/m² leaves through the convective tip; the remaining 8,4898{,}489 W/m² conducts out through the fixed-temperature wall at x=0x=0.

Solver cross-check

The Thomas algorithm is compared with numpy.linalg.solve on 64 seeded random diagonally dominant 64×6464\times64 systems. The worst absolute difference is 1.1×10−161.1\times10^{-16} — machine precision.

Limitations

  • One-dimensional, steady state; no transients, no fins with varying cross-section, no multi-dimensional spreading.
  • Constant thermal properties; temperature-dependent k(T)k(T) would make the problem nonlinear.
  • No contact resistance at either boundary.
  • Radiation from the tip is excluded; including the nonlinear T4T^4 term would require linearisation or iteration.
  • The manufactured convergence case is a verification device, not a physical scenario; it exists because the physical exact solution is quadratic and is reproduced to roundoff.
