Pipe Flow Sizing: verified hydraulics before a real loop exists

Objective

Written in the third month after moving from software engineering into mechanical engineering, this study builds the smallest useful hydraulic tool: size a pipe run, total a series network, and find where a pump actually operates. The objective was not capability — handbooks provide that — but verified foundations. Every friction factor, drop, and operating point the later FSAE cooling study would use had to trace back to an identity, an implicit-equation residual, or a published accuracy band before being trusted.

Five checks were predeclared:

  1. the laminar branch reproduces f=64/Ref=64/Re to machine precision;
  2. the Newton Colebrook solution drives the implicit residual below 10−1210^{-12} across a Reynolds/roughness grid;
  3. turbulent friction factors agree with the explicit Swamee–Jain formula inside its published ±3%\pm3\% band, and the deviation is reported rather than hidden;
  4. the pump/system intersection solves with residual below 10−1010^{-10} Pa and lands inside an independent brute-force bracket;
  5. the series-network total drop equals the sum of the per-element drops exactly.

Method

Friction factor

For incompressible flow in a circular pipe, the Darcy–Weisbach head loss is

Δp=(fLD+∑K)ρV22,Re=ρVDμ,\Delta p = \left(f\,\frac{L}{D} + \sum K\right)\frac{\rho V^2}{2}, \qquad Re=\frac{\rho V D}{\mu},

with ff the Darcy friction factor and ∑K\sum K the summed minor-loss coefficients of the element. Below the critical Reynolds number Re=2300Re=2300 the analytical solution f=64/Ref=64/Re is used. Above it, the Colebrook equation

1f=−2log⁡10(ε3.7D+2.51Ref)\frac{1}{\sqrt{f}} = -2\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{3.7D} + \frac{2.51}{Re\,\sqrt{f}}\right)

is implicit in ff. It is solved by Newton iteration on x=1/fx=1/\sqrt{f},

g(x)=x+2log⁡10(ε3.7D+2.51xRe),g′(x)=1+2ln⁡102.51/Reε/3.7D+2.51x/Re,g(x)=x+2\log_{10}\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{3.7D}+\frac{2.51\,x}{Re}\right),\qquad g'(x)=1+\frac{2}{\ln 10}\,\frac{2.51/Re}{\varepsilon/3.7D+2.51\,x/Re},

starting from the explicit Haaland estimate [1]. Quadratic convergence from that guess reaches the residual gate in a handful of iterations; a 50-iteration guard turns non-convergence into a loud failure rather than a silent bad factor. The explicit Swamee–Jain formula [2],

f=0.25[log⁡10(ε3.7D+5.74Re0.9)]2,f=\frac{0.25}{\left[\log_{10}\!\left(\dfrac{\varepsilon}{3.7D}+\dfrac{5.74}{Re^{0.9}}\right)\right]^2},

serves as the independent cross-check over its published validity range 5×103≤Re≤1085\times10^3\le Re\le10^8, 10−6≤ε/D≤10−210^{-6}\le\varepsilon/D\le10^{-2}.

Series network

A series network shares one flow rate QQ through every element, so the total drop is the sum of element drops and, given an available pressure, the flow follows from a monotone bisection on ∑Δpi(Q)=Δpavail\sum \Delta p_i(Q)=\Delta p_\mathrm{avail}. Monotonicity of the system curve guarantees a unique bracket.

Pump operating point

The pump is a declared quadratic model, not vendor data:

Δppump(Q)=p0−cQ2,\Delta p_\mathrm{pump}(Q)=p_0-c\,Q^2,

intersected with the system resistance curve Δpsys(Q)=pstatic+∑Δpi(Q)\Delta p_\mathrm{sys}(Q)=p_\mathrm{static}+\sum\Delta p_i(Q) by Newton iteration with a central-difference derivative, backstopped by bisection on the guaranteed bracket [0,Qmax][0, Q_\mathrm{max}]. The example loop is a representative FSAE-scale cooling circuit — suction hose, radiator core, engine gallery, return hose — with declared water properties (ρ=998.2\rho=998.2 kg/m³, μ=1.0016×10−3\mu=1.0016\times10^{-3} Pa·s), declared K coefficients, and declared pump coefficients p0=90p_0=90 kPa, c=2.025×1011c=2.025\times10^{11} Pa·s²/m⁶ (40 L/min free delivery). Every coefficient is arbitrary-but-declared: the point is the verified intersection machinery, not a specific product.

Verification

The acceptance runner (scripts/analyse.py) regenerates every number and exits nonzero unless all gates pass:

The laminar branch is a direct evaluation of the identity, so its zero error is exact, not approximate. The Colebrook residual measures the solved ff against the defining equation itself. The Swamee–Jain comparison is the only check against an external published result; restricting it to the formula’s declared validity range matters, because the deviation exceeds 3% outside it (for example at Re=4×103Re=4\times10^3 with very low roughness), and the runner reports that rather than silently widening the grid.

Limitations

  • Steady, incompressible, single-phase flow. No transients, no compressibility, no air entrainment.
  • No cavitation model: the pump curve is followed wherever the mathematics goes, and suction-side vapour pressure is never checked.
  • Minor-loss K coefficients are declared handbook order-of-magnitude values; published K tables carry wide uncertainty, and real fittings vary with manufacture and Reynolds number.
  • The pump curve is a declared quadratic, not vendor data; a real curve bends near shutoff and free delivery, and real pumps have efficiency and NPSH limits this model cannot express.
  • The laminar/turbulent transition is a hard switch at Re=2300Re=2300; the transition band is not modelled.
  • The network solver handles series topology only; parallel branches need a junction-pressure iteration this study deliberately did not build yet.
