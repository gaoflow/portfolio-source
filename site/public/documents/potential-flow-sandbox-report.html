Potential Flow Sandbox: complex potentials, superposition, and circulation

Objective

Before trusting panel methods or vortex-lattice codes, the underlying potential-flow machinery has to be exact. This study implements the classical elementary flows with a complex-potential formulation, superposes them into flow past a circular cylinder with and without circulation, and verifies every quantity that has an analytical answer. It asks five concrete questions:

  1. Does the velocity field reproduce the surface pressure coefficient Cp=1−4sin⁡2θC_p = 1 - 4\sin^2\theta?
  2. Do the stagnation points with circulation follow sin⁡θ=−Γ/(4πUR)\sin\theta = -\Gamma/(4\pi U R)?
  3. Does a contour integral of the computed velocity field recover the imposed circulation?
  4. Do RK4-traced streamlines stay on the analytical streamfunction level?
  5. Does the integrated surface pressure reproduce the Kutta–Joukowski lift L′=ρUΓL' = \rho U \Gamma in sign and magnitude?

Method: complex potentials and superposition

Each elementary flow is written as a complex potential W(z)W(z) with z=x+iyz = x + iy, and the physical velocity follows from

dWdz=u−iv.\frac{dW}{dz} = u - iv.

The implemented primitives are the uniform stream W=UzW = Uz, the source/sink W=m2πln⁡zW = \frac{m}{2\pi}\ln z, the doublet W=μ2πzW = \frac{\mu}{2\pi z}, and the point vortex W=iΓ2πln⁡zW = \frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln z. Circulation uses the aerodynamics sign convention: positive Γ\Gamma circulates clockwise.

Superposing the uniform stream, the doublet of moment μ=2πUR2\mu = 2\pi U R^2, and the vortex gives flow past a circular cylinder of radius RR with circulation:

W(z)=U(z+R2z)+iΓ2πln⁡z.W(z) = U\!\left(z + \frac{R^2}{z}\right) + \frac{i\Gamma}{2\pi}\ln z .

Because the governing equation is linear, the superposition is exact; there is no modelling error to calibrate, only implementation error to catch. The streamfunction is ψ=Im⁡W\psi = \operatorname{Im} W and Bernoulli gives the surface pressure coefficient Cp=1−(V/U)2C_p = 1 - (V/U)^2.

Verification cases

All cases use U=1U = 1, R=1R = 1, ρ=1.225\rho = 1.225, and the lift case Γ=2πUR\Gamma = 2\pi U R, which places the stagnation points at sin⁡θ=−12\sin\theta = -\tfrac{1}{2}, i.e. θ=−30∘\theta = -30^\circ and −150∘-150^\circ.

Surface pressure. Evaluating |dW/dz||dW/dz| on r=Rr = R at 4097 stations reproduces Cp=1−4sin⁡2θC_p = 1 - 4\sin^2\theta with a maximum absolute error of 2.66×10−152.66\times10^{-15} against a 10−1210^{-12} gate. The same evaluation with circulation matches the analytical Cp(θ)=1−(2sin⁡θ+Γ/(2πRU))2C_p(\theta) = 1 - (2\sin\theta + \Gamma/(2\pi R U))^2 to the same scale.

Stagnation points. Bracketing and bisecting the signed tangential surface speed recovers −30.00000000000005∘-30.00000000000005^\circ and −149.99999999999997∘-149.99999999999997^\circ: a maximum angle error of 8.9×10−168.9\times10^{-16} rad against a 10−910^{-9} rad gate.

Circulation recovery. A 4096-point trapezoid line integral of the velocity field around a circle of radius 2.5R2.5R returns Γ=6.283185307179585\Gamma = 6.283185307179585 against the imposed 6.2831853071795866.283185307179586: an absolute error of 8.9×10−168.9\times10^{-16} against a 10−910^{-9} gate. The uniform and doublet parts contribute zero circulation, so this isolates the vortex term.

Streamline tracing. A fixed-step classical RK4 integrator marches along the unit velocity field from (−3,1.2)(-3, 1.2) around the cylinder. At step h=0.01h = 0.01 the maximum drift of ψ\psi from its initial analytical value is 2.56×10−122.56\times10^{-12} against a 10−610^{-6} gate, and halving the step reduces the drift by a factor of 16.0: an observed convergence order of 4.003, confirming fourth-order behaviour.

Kutta–Joukowski lift. Integrating the Bernoulli surface pressure over 8192 stations gives L′=7.696902001294994L' = 7.696902001294994 per unit span, matching ρUΓ\rho U \Gamma with zero relative error at the integration precision, the correct (upward) sign, and zero drag to machine precision — d’Alembert’s paradox retained as an explicit property of the model.

Twelve behavioural unit tests additionally pin the elementary-flow hand values, surface impermeability, the far-field decay, the circulation sign convention, and the fourth-order convergence of the tracer.

Limitations

  • The model is inviscid, irrotational, and incompressible; there is no boundary layer, no separation, and no wake.
  • Circulation is imposed, not predicted: a real lifting cylinder sheds vorticity, and the Kutta condition that selects Γ\Gamma on an airfoil has no analogue here.
  • The flow is strictly two-dimensional; nothing is said about spanwise effects or finite bodies.
  • The zero-drag result is a property of the model class, not a prediction.
